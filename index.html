About this Survey — PIANC Working Group 263
Context and Objectives
This survey is part of the work of PIANC InCom Working Group 263 — Key Elements and Best Practices to Improve the Governance of Inland Waterways. The WG was established in recognition of a lack of consolidated and comparative information on how different countries, provinces, and regional blocs manage their inland waterway infrastructure. Governance and management approaches vary significantly across the world, shaped by geographical boundaries, political structures, river basin configurations, economic communities, and bilateral or multilateral agreements.

The primary objective of WG 263 is to discuss and compare waterway governance and management practices across a wide range of countries and regions — examining different forms of inland waterway infrastructure, the respective measures and instruments adopted, and how they relate to the key elements of governance. The WG will seek to identify patterns, recurrent practices, and possible reference standards for different types of waterway systems, while evaluating the applicability of approaches employed by international commissions such as the CCNR, Danube Commission, Mekong River Commission, and others.

Important: Scope and Limits of this Research
This survey is a descriptive and comparative instrument. It does not seek to judge, rank, or criticize any particular governance model or public policy approach. The diversity of practices across countries reflects the perceived scale, history, and specific conditions of each waterway system. The aim is not to impose a single standard, but to understand how different regions of the world address issues inherent to inland waterway infrastructure — and, on that basis, to provide a framework that may serve as a reference for those developing new waterway management programmes or seeking to improve existing ones. Responses will be treated with full confidentiality and used exclusively for research purposes within the scope of the WG 263 report.
